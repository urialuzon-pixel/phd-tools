RESEARCH PROPOSAL
Geopolitical Impunity and Extractive Activity:
A Hybrid Physics–ML Framework for Detecting Mining Anomalies in Disputed Territories
PhD Research Proposal  |  Data Science & Environmental Geopolitics

Core Research Proposition
Contested sovereignty creates enforcement vacuums that enable illegal extraction.
We can detect and quantify this effect by combining a physical hydrological model
with satellite observations — and using ML to learn what the physics alone cannot explain.
The residual between physical prediction and observed reality is where geopolitics lives.

Research Question
Do territories with active sovereignty disputes exhibit systematic excess extractive activity — deforestation, mining, sediment pollution — beyond what natural hydrological and environmental conditions predict?

The secondary question: Is the magnitude of that excess statistically predicted by geopolitical features — dispute status, governance quality, metal prices, proximity to borders?

Model Architecture
Step 1 — Physical Baseline Model
A hydrological sediment transport model (SWAT or a simplified mass-balance routing model) predicts expected water turbidity and sediment load at downstream river monitoring points, given only natural inputs:
•	Precipitation (ERA5 reanalysis, free, global, 1979–present)
•	Digital elevation model — slope and catchment geometry (SRTM 30m, free)
•	Soil type and erodibility (FAO HWSD, free)
•	Baseline land cover in the absence of human disturbance (ESA CCI Land Cover)

Output: predicted turbidity time series ŷ_physics at each catchment outlet.

Step 2 — Satellite Observation
Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery is used to compute observed turbidity via NDTI (Normalized Difference Turbidity Index) and suspended sediment proxies at the same downstream locations.

Output: observed turbidity time series y_observed, 2017–present at 10m resolution.

Step 3 — Residual Computation
The residual is defined as:

residual(t, location) = y_observed(t) − ŷ_physics(t)

Positive residuals = more sediment/turbidity than natural conditions predict. This excess is the anomaly signal — the fingerprint of human activity the physics did not account for.

Step 4 — ML Residual Predictor
A gradient boosting model (XGBoost / LightGBM) or a spatiotemporal LSTM is trained to predict the residual using geopolitical and economic covariates:
•	Dispute status of the catchment zone (binary + intensity score from ICOW)
•	Governance quality index (World Bank WGI — Rule of Law, Control of Corruption)
•	Distance to nearest international border
•	Global metal price index (gold, copper, tin — World Bank Pink Sheet)
•	Conflict event density in the zone (ACLED, lagged)
•	Forest loss in upstream catchment (Hansen/GFW annual data)

If dispute status is a significant positive predictor of the residual, we have quantitative evidence that geopolitical impunity drives excess extraction — beyond what commodity prices or governance alone explain.

Step 5 — Causal Validation
To move from correlation to causation, an event study design is applied around dispute escalation events (ICJ filings, militarized incidents from MID dataset). Does the residual spike following escalation? Does it persist? This tests the mechanism, not just the association.

Techniques & Tools
Physical Modeling
•	SWAT (Soil & Water Assessment Tool) — full hydrological model if calibration data allows
•	Simplified mass-balance sediment routing as fallback for data-sparse regions
•	Google Earth Engine (GEE) — satellite data processing at scale

Remote Sensing & Feature Engineering
•	NDTI / NDWI band ratio computation from Sentinel-2 for turbidity estimation
•	Change detection on Hansen/GFW annual layers for deforestation signal
•	Spatial joins: catchment delineation from DEM using pysheds or WhiteboxTools

Machine Learning
•	XGBoost / LightGBM for tabular residual prediction with SHAP interpretability
•	Spatiotemporal LSTM or Transformer for sequence modeling of residual time series
•	Propensity Score Matching for disputed vs. non-disputed zone comparison

Causal Inference
•	Event study / interrupted time series around dispute escalation dates
•	Difference-in-differences with matched control catchments
•	Spatial lag models to control for geographic autocorrelation (PySAL)

Software Stack
•	Python — primary: rasterio, geopandas, xarray, pysheds, scikit-learn, shap
•	R — optional: plm (panel models), spdep (spatial econometrics)
•	Google Earth Engine (JavaScript / Python API) — satellite data at scale
•	QGIS — spatial validation and visual inspection

Known Limitations to Address in the Literature Review
•	SWAT calibration requires stream gauge data — sparse in contested zones. May need to fall back to uncalibrated or regionalized parameters. Assess the literature on ungauged basin estimation.
•	Turbidity detects surface/alluvial mining only — underground hard-rock mines leave no water signal. Scope must be clearly bounded.
•	Dispute databases (ICOW, MID) have variable temporal granularity. Check coverage before committing to the event study design.
•	Confounding: disputed zones may differ geologically from non-disputed zones (more mineral-rich, hence disputed in the first place). This endogeneity must be addressed explicitly.

Research Novelty
The primary methodological contribution is the hybrid pipeline itself:
physics model → residual → ML geopolitical predictor.

Most remote sensing studies of illegal extraction use pure ML or pure change detection.
Most geopolitical studies of resource extraction use aggregate country-level statistics.

This proposal operates at catchment resolution, over time, with an interpretable
physical baseline — and frames the residual as a geopolitically meaningful signal.
That framing is, to the best of current knowledge, novel.
